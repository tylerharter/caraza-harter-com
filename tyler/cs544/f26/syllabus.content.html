Syllabus

Welcome to Intro to Big Data Systems! We'll deploy and use distributed systems to store and analyze large datasets. Unstructured and structured approaches to storage will be covered. Analysis will involve learning new query languages, processing streaming data, and training machine learning models. Systems covered include Docker, PyTorch, HDFS, Spark, Cassandra, Kafka, and more.

Learning Objectives

  • Deploy distributed systems for data storage and analytics
  • Demonstrate competencies with tools and processes necessary for loading data into distributed storage systems
  • Write programs that use distributed platforms to efficiently analyze large datasets
  • Produce meaning from large datasets by training machine learning models in parallel or on distributed systems
  • Measure resource usage and overall cost of running distributed programs
  • Optimize distributed analytics programs to reduce resource consumption and program runtime
  • Demonstrate competencies with cloud services designed to store or analyze large datasets

Lecture

We meet 3 times a week -- see the lecture schedule here.

Lecture attendance is expected. Attendance is not graded directly; instead, we'll do graded hand-in worksheets during class on days that are not announced in advance. Because only your best 7 worksheets count, you have room to miss several lectures for illness, travel, or anything else. This is automatic -- you do not need to notify me when you miss a lecture, and there are no makeups for a worksheet you missed.

Readings

We'll be learning about many different big data systems, and so no textbook closely corresponds to the lecture content. Thus, attending lectures and taking notes will be your primary resource.

We will have recommended (though optional) readings for many systems, however. We'll select from O'Reilly text books because you can read them free online via the Madison Public Library. You just need to do the following:

  1. get a library card (free)
  2. sign into the O'Reilly collection with your card number
  3. search for the assigned book

Here are some of the main texts we'll reference this semester:

  • Designing Data-Intensive Applications: The Big Ideas Behind Reliable, Scalable, and Maintainable Systems (1st edition), by Martin Kleppmann
  • Learning Spark: Lightning-Fast Data Analytics (2nd edition), by Jules Damji et al.
  • Cassandra: The Definitive Guide, (Revised) Third Edition: Distributed Data at Web Scale 3rd Edition (3rd edition), by Jeff Carpenter et al.
  • Kafka: The Definitive Guide: Real-Time Data and Stream Processing at Scale (2nd Edition), by Gwen Shapira et al.

Quizzes and Final Exam

The final exam and all quizzes will be cumulative, and closed books/notes/AI/Internet. The quizzes will be administered via the new computer-based evaluation facility (CBEF), so you will be able to signup for a quiz time that works well for you.

The final exam will be Friday, December 11, 2026, 2:45-4:45 PM (location TBD).

The final exam cannot be skipped, nor taken before the regular time. It may only be rescheduled to a later date (with instructor permission), if necessary.

Policy for missed quizzes:

  • You are expected to take all quizzes unless you are ill or have another valid reason not to take a quiz (e.g., travel).
  • If you cannot take a quiz for a valid reason, simply don't take it. There is no process where I will try to verify whether a reason is legitimate (verification is impractical in the age of AI).
  • If you miss 1 quiz, your final will become worth 38 points; if you miss 2, it will be worth 46 points.
  • If you need to miss more than 2 quizzes for any reason, you are encouraged to take the course a different semester (e.g., you may want to ask an advisor about Medical Withdrawals). We will not provide makeups or reweighting beyond the first two missed quizzes.
  • Once you've started taking a quiz, you cannot drop it after the fact.

Academic misconduct policy:

  • you may only study/prep for a quiz/exam with other students who have NOT yet taken it. Unless you have first-hand knowledge that another student has taken an assessment, assume they have not taken it
  • after taking an assessment, do not discuss questions or answers with other students until grades have been released
  • you may not sit adjacent to anybody you have met or know (even slightly) during the final

Hand-in Worksheets

We will do 10 graded worksheets during class time. These will involve answering questions and illustrating systems. The best 7 of these will count towards your grade. We will not announce in advance which days we will do worksheets. I'll bring the sheets so you don't need to print.

The worksheets must be handed in at the end of lecture (later submissions are not accepted).

You may lose points on worksheets for: illegible name or writing, using staples/paperclips/glue, wrong answers, or paper that is crumpled or torn (these can jam the scanner).

You may discuss with other students IN PERSON on the worksheets. If you do, you will still submit individually. You MAY NOT use AI on worksheets.
